How to Get Your Husband to Help Around the House (Ask for This Instead)

Sarah Showers · August 5, 2026 · 4 min read

If you've searched "how to get my husband to help around the house," you've probably already tried the obvious things: asking nicely, asking not-so-nicely, the chore chart, the app, the calm Sunday conversation that lasted about four days. Here's the uncomfortable reframe that actually changes things: the goal was never to get him to help more. Help is the problem.

Why "helping" never sticks

When your partner "helps," you're still the manager. You noticed the task, remembered it, decided when it should happen, and assigned it. He executed one item off a list only you can see. So the load, the noticing and tracking and deciding, never actually moves. Which is why it always drifts back to you within a week: you were never really off the hook, you just temporarily delegated.

A partner who helps is a partner who waits to be told. And being the one who always has to tell is the exhausting part.

Ask him to own something instead

The shift is from help to ownership. Instead of handing off tasks, hand off a whole domain, an entire area he owns end to end, including remembering that it needs doing.

  • Task thinking: "Can you take out the bins tonight?" (You still track bin day forever.)
  • Ownership thinking: "Bins and recycling are yours now, all of it, including knowing when." (It leaves your head entirely.)

Pick a few areas that eat your week, anything medical, the car, meals, one kid's activities, and give each a single owner. Not a helper. An owner who is now the person who notices, because it's theirs.

The full script for having that conversation without it turning into a fight is here: how to divide the mental load with your partner.

Let him do it his own way

This is the part that makes or breaks it. When he owns something, he'll do it differently than you would, and probably drop a ball or two at first. If you swoop in and correct or take it back, you've just taught both of you that it's still really yours. Ownership only transfers if you let it be imperfect. A domain he genuinely owns, mistakes included, beats one you're still quietly supervising.

Make it visible, not nagged

Write the ownership down where you can both see it, so it's a shared fact instead of a thing you have to keep re-raising. The point isn't to police him. It's that the household stops running entirely through you.

And if the conversation keeps stalling or sliding back, a Your Family Brief session gives you a neutral, structured way to divide it up with your real list, so it sticks past next Tuesday.

You don't need your husband to help more. You need to not be the only one who remembers. That's a bigger ask, and it's the one that actually works.
